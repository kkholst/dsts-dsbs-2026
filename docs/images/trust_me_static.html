Why math

Professional mathematics is a perfect combination of

  • fully digital existence
  • correctness is objectively verifiable
  • and a large body of high-quality training material

With Lean, mathematical results are fully formalizable and proofs are machine verifiable.

The capability question

What is settled
  • AI systems can be capable of solving highly complex problems
  • e.g. doing research, possibly with superhuman capabilities
  • and sometimes with scary and dangerous side effects (Hugging Face incident etc.)
What is unsettled
  • The actual business model
  • If the resource tradeoff is acceptable
  • What we can achieve with small and local models
  • Whether the development generalizes to all human capabilities

17 November: a new study shows that coding agents report p<0.05 in between 54% and 98% of simulated null examples.

arXiv:2611.23174

Is the statistics profession affected?

Substantially on
  • mathematical derivations and proofs
  • coding for data analysis, visualization, reporting
  • design of studies and methodology selection
At the moment less on
  • which questions are worth asking
  • who is ultimately accountable
  • oversight / regulation

What is next?

The developments will

  • spread across all disciplines
  • decrease the value of replaceable production skills
  • increase productivity (hopefully in terms of quality rather than quantity)
  • with dramatic effects on the job market and education system

Existential crisis

Programmers, whose identity is writing excellent code

Mathematicians, whose identity is solving difficult problems

Statisticians, whose identity is producing excellent analyses

Production capabilities are greatly devalued when tools can take over.

Is there a version where human value is not attached to productivity?

Some positions among mathematicians

The skeptical
  • AI is ruining mathematics (leave our field alone)
  • Rejection of AI on individual grounds
  • The Association for Human Mathematics

AHM, October 2026: 1,008 members; 654 of them pledge to do research without AI.

The cautious but open minded
  • Goals of AI companies are misaligned with those of scientific communities
  • AI is a new game-changing instrument: the particle accelerator of mathematics
  • Terence Tao: Math 2.0

Tao, Caltech lecture, October 2026: from a scarcity of proofs to an abundance of proofs.

The fragile position

Humans are needed because AI cannot

  • write good papers
  • do “truly” creative thinking
  • have human judgment
  • avoid post-selection inference fallacies
  • X

Until recently, few mathematicians believed that AI could solve open research problems.

The central question

Do we want a world where no human, not even in principle, can follow and verify a claim or decision made by AI?

3

The defendable position

Two roles that we should claim

  • Regulation (what can AI do?)
  • Oversight (does AI comply?)

Implementation of regulation and oversight is a hot topic; it is unsettled if it is even possible with a superhuman AI.

Requires institutional support and the overseer’s possibility to reject.

Two other roles—if we want them

We (humans) can insist on the following roles:

  • Framing: what is the problem we want to solve
  • Valuation: tradeoffs between values with no fixed exchange rate

My position—Humans should maintain agency

The p<0.05 claim must be:
  • We should demand transparency and decision making that can be followed, justified, questioned and challenged
  • justified through a transparent analysis, all the way
  • We should have the right to frame what the relevant question is
  • the answer to a question that we actually want to ask
  • We should form our own preferences, opinions and values
  • a claim that we have the right to contest

We do not roll over and do not accept claims by authority alone, only if they can be transparently supported.

Value is detached from productivity and attached to maintaining agency.

Outlook

Research expansion

There is an infinity of unsolved problems!

  • Accelerated development (proof abundance)
  • Unprecedented interdisciplinary research
  • Development of new research fields previously impossible
  • Human researchers may take roles as: framers, referees, research journalists, or spectators and surveyors.
Risks
  • End of humanity
  • Severe disruptions of societal structures
  • Unemployment
  • Alienation
  • AI vs Human credit disputes
  • Broken publication model and assignment of merit
  • Market mechanisms alone may not grant humans any roles

Education

To ensure competence in our roles we need:
  • Education to continue as an institutional structure
  • Humans to maintain the framing role of education
  • Less tool training (PROC TTEST, t.test())
  • More enlightenment (what do H0 and p<0.05 actually mean)
  • Sharper focus on limitations and breakdown points
  • Increased focus on adversarial behavior
  • Critical assessment over productivity tooling
Risks
  • uncertain job market connection
  • individual incentives are credentials, not agency
  • uncertain human educator roles
  • what we actually want is more difficult to measure
